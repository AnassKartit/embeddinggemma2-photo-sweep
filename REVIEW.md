# EmbeddingGemma 2 for on-device photo cleanup: measured review

All numbers: one run on 12 October 2026, MacBook Pro M4 Pro 24 GB, GPU shared with desktop apps (60-70% busy before the runs, so
speeds are pessimistic). Raw results are in `results/` and `decide/`; every table names the script that made it.

## Test set: 158 photos

Every photo is re-saved the way WhatsApp does (long side 1600 px, JPEG quality 70) before any model sees it.

| Group | n | Truth | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your family | 29 | keep | Codex, one fictional American family of 5 from a reference sheet, new clothes in every scene |
| Other families | 12 | not family | Codex, two other fictional families in the same look (the hardest negatives) |
| Strangers | 40 | not family | real photos, Flickr30k test split (fetched by script, not shipped) |
| Forwards, greetings, memes, quotes | 22 | junk | Codex, typical family-group forwards (fictional brands) |
| Screenshots | 20 | junk | mobile web pages + chat screens, headless Chrome (regenerated by script) |
| Receipts | 25 | junk | real, CORD-v2 test split (fetched by script) |
| Accidental shots | 10 | junk | pocket/black frames and blurred floors |

## 1. EmbeddingGemma 2 on the cleanup tasks (`bench/bench.py`)

| | 280 image tokens (library default) | 70 image tokens (Google's on-device budget) |
|---|---|---|
| Speed, Mac GPU (MPS) | 556 ms/photo | **85 ms/photo** |
| Zero-shot: your family photos kept | 93.1% | **100%** |
| Zero-shot: junk kept by mistake | 2 of 77 | 1 of 77 |
| Zero-shot: receipts / screenshots / accidental caught | 100% / 70% / 40% | 100% / 80% / 40% |
| Near-duplicates: lowest real-duplicate cosine vs highest different-photo cosine | 0.951 vs 0.885 | 0.918 vs 0.896 |
| Text search, right photo in the top 5 | 8 / 10 | 8 / 10 |

Zero-shot means no training: each pile is a few plain-English labels ("a screenshot of a phone screen"). Its weak spot: 21 of the
40 real stranger photos were sorted as screenshots or forwards (wrong pile, but still not kept as family).
Storage per photo: 3 KB at 768 float32, 128 bytes at 128 dims int8. 256 dims scored the same as 768.

## 2. Clustering with no labels (`bench/cluster.py`)

k-means with k = 7, scored against the true groups (1.0 = perfect):

| | ARI | NMI | purity | family photos in one cluster | that cluster is family |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 70 tokens, 768 dims | 0.853 | 0.866 | 0.905 | 96.6% | 93.3% |
| 70 tokens, 256 dims | 0.866 | 0.892 | 0.911 | 96.6% | 93.3% |
| 280 tokens, 768 dims | 0.870 | 0.869 | 0.911 | 100% | 93.5% |

Average-linkage clustering did badly (ARI 0.24 to 0.33): use k-means. Part of the family-vs-other-family split is setting and look
(American vs Moroccan scenes), not identity.

## 3. Find the whole family from ONE photo (`bench/oneshot.py`, `bench/faces.py`)

EmbeddingGemma 2 sees "a family scene", not who is in it: from one photo it put 24 of the 29 family photos in its top 29 (AUC 0.978).
Face matching does the identity part: YuNet finds faces (MIT), SFace compares them (Apache 2.0), 19 ms/photo on CPU.

One ordinary family scene with 3+ faces as the only example, averaged over all 22 such scenes:

| Faces rule | your family kept | other families kept | strangers | junk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cos >= 0.5, any face | **97.4%** | **0.8%** | 0% | 0% |
| cos >= 0.45, any face | 98.9% | 5.7% | 0% | 0% |
| cos >= 0.45, >= 2 faces | 95.9% | 0% | 0% | 0% |

With SFace's published threshold (0.363) other families were kept 43% of the time from one ordinary family photo (`bench/faces.py`, k=1) and 75% from the reference sheet: Codex faces are more alike than real ones
(median face similarity 0.16 between families vs 0.06 for real strangers). The 0.5 rule was chosen on this data: confirm it on a fresh
set before shipping.

## 4. Decision models vs EmbeddingGemma 2: junk or keep (`decide/`)

Question per photo: "Is this image junk the user can delete, not a personal photo of people?" Truth: 77 junk, 81 photos of people.

Which OpenRouter decision models can read an image at all (`decide/probe.py`, `decide/control.py`, 3 junk + 3 family controls):
- **Read images:** OpenAI GPT-6 Luna Decisions, Perplexity Decider V1.1 27B, Cloudflare Clef, Clef Omni, Inception Mercury Decide, Liquid d1.
- **Accept an image but cannot see it** (same score on junk and family): Clef Flash, Nace Drex 1.5, Upstage Solar Decide (both), Together Tev1 4B.
- **Reject images** (the image is read as text and overflows the context): Microsoft Decision-1, TypeSafe Jev 1.13, Kev 4B.

All 158 photos, 512 px JPEG sent to the cloud (`decide/full.py`, $0.19 for 948 calls):

| | accuracy | junk caught | people photos wrongly junked | median time | cost / 1000 photos | photos leave the device |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Perplexity Decider V1.1 27B | **100%** | 77/77 | 0/81 | 0.44 s | $0.006 | yes |
| Inception Mercury Decide (free) | **100%** | 77/77 | 0/81 | 0.44 s | $0 | yes |
| Liquid d1 | 99.4% | 76/77 | 0/81 | 0.50 s | $0.014 | yes |
| OpenAI GPT-6 Luna Decisions | 98.1% | 75/77 | 1/81 | 0.36 s | $0.038 | yes |
| Cloudflare Clef Omni | 94.9% | 69/77 | 0/81 | 0.37 s | $0.054 | yes |
| Cloudflare Clef | 86.7% | 56/77 | 0/81 | 1.44 s | $1.09 | yes |
| EmbeddingGemma 2, zero-shot | 86.1% | 76/77 | 21/81 | **0.085 s** | $0 | **no** |
| EmbeddingGemma 2 + you tag 20 photos (`decide/tagged.json`) | 96.9% | 96.3% | 2.6% | **0.085 s** | $0 | **no** |
| EmbeddingGemma 2 + you tag 40 photos | 97.8% | 96.6% | 1.1% | **0.085 s** | $0 | **no** |

The tagged rows are 50 random draws of k junk + k keep photos, a logistic regression on the 768-dim vectors, scored on the rest (`decide/tagged.py`; table: `decide/summary.py`).

## Verdict

- **Best accuracy:** a decision model that reads images (Perplexity Decider, Mercury Decide). But every photo is uploaded.
- **Best private option:** EmbeddingGemma 2 on the device. Zero-shot it never lost a family photo and caught 76 of 77 junk; tag 20
  photos and it reaches 96.9% on junk-or-keep, about 4x faster than the fastest cloud call (0.085 s vs 0.36 s), $0, offline.
- **Family needs faces:** EmbeddingGemma 2 plus YuNet/SFace finds 97.4% of the family from one photo.
- **Duplicates and search come free** from the same vectors.
- **Never auto-delete.** The app sorts into piles; the user confirms; Undo puts files back.

## Pipeline used by the app (`app/engine.py`)

1. EmbeddingGemma 2, 70 image tokens, 256 dims, cached per file.
2. Faces: YuNet + SFace; one family photo enrols the faces; keep if any face matches at cos >= 0.5.
3. Piles: Family (keep), Duplicates (cos >= 0.93, keep the biggest copy), Forwards & memes, Screenshots, Receipts & documents,
   Blurry & accidental (zero-shot + darkness/sharpness check), Other people.
4. Search box in the same embedding space.

On phones: Google ships `embeddinggemma-2-text-vision-440m.litertlm` (388 MB, int8 vision) for MediaPipe `UniversalEmbedder`
on iOS and Android; YuNet and SFace run through OpenCV. Plan and risks: `docs/mobile-feasibility.md`.

## 5. Ask your files: search a Downloads folder by meaning, answer locally (`rag/`)

Test folder: `rag/make_downloads.py`, 62 fictional files for one made-up person (three electricity bills from 2024, 2025 and 2026 with matching
dates and dull file names, other bills, a boarding pass, a lease, notes, installers, family photos, screenshots, receipts, forwards). 30 plain questions with the right
file written down first (`rag/eval.py`, results/ask_eval.json). EmbeddingGemma 2 indexes document chunks
("title: <file> | text: <chunk>"), photos (70 image tokens) and bare file names into one space; "latest/newest" in a question
sorts near-ties by date.

| 30 questions | right file first | in the top 5 |
|---|---|---|
| **EmbeddingGemma 2** | **30** | **30** |
| file-name search (any word of the question in the name) | 12 | 15 |
| macOS Spotlight, keywords only (`mdfind`) | 11 | 12 |
| macOS Spotlight, the question as typed | 5 | 6 |

62 files indexed in 5.0 s, median 34 ms per question (2026-10-13; the GPU was about 70% busy with other apps). The newest bill is
saved as `ebill_download.pdf`, so only its date says it is the latest; "where's my latest electricity bill?" still found it first.
(An earlier version of this folder had three CVs instead of the bills: 29/30, Spotlight 6, file names 13.)

Then a small local model reads only the top 3 files (with their dates) and answers in one sentence, naming the file
(`rag/answer.py`, 11 questions whose answer is inside a document, right answers written first). Photos have no text, so a photo
match is answered directly ("Here it is: IMG_1029.jpg").

| Answer model (MLX, 4-bit, this Mac) | correct | peak memory | median answer time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Gemma 4 E4B (QAT) | 11/11 | 6.5 GB | 2.0 s |
| Qwen3 4B Instruct 2507 | 11/11 | 3.0 GB | 2.2 s |
| Qwen3.5 9B | 11/11 | 5.9 GB | 4.8 s |
| Gemma 4 12B (QAT) | did not load: mlx-lm 0.31.3 and 0.32.0 do not support `gemma4_unified` | | |

Times are from 2026-10-13 with the GPU about 70% busy with other apps (an earlier run on a quieter Mac: 1.0 s, 0.8 s, 1.4 s).
One question is "how much was my latest electricity bill?": all three answered $131.44 from the newest file. With the right
files found first, a 4B model is enough. Related project: DigUp (github.com/ARahim3/DigUp) also searches a Mac's files
with EmbeddingGemma 2; other local RAG projects and vector stores: `docs/local-rag-research.md`.

## 6. Do you need a vector database? (`rag/stores.py`)

Same EmbeddingGemma 2 vectors and questions, four places to keep them. Part 1: the real folder (run on the earlier CV version of
it, 30 questions). Part 2: 50,000 random 768-number vectors grouped around 300 centres, recall of the true top 10 (exact numpy).
The Mac was rendering video during part 2, so times are upper bounds.

| Store | real folder: right file first | 50k: median query | 50k: recall@10 | 50k: build |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| numpy in memory | 29/30, under 1 ms | 6.1 ms | 1.00 | 0 s |
| ChromaDB 1.5.9 (HNSW) | 29/30, under 1 ms | 10.7 ms | 0.92 | 11.5 s |
| sqlite-vec 0.1.6 (exact) | 29/30, under 1 ms | 95 ms | 1.00 | 2.8 s |
| Postgres 17 + pgvector, exact | 29/30, under 1 ms | 323 ms | 1.00 | 9.7 s |
| Postgres 17 + pgvector, HNSW (defaults) | 29/30, under 1 ms | 21.9 ms | 0.636 | 285 s |

For one person's folder the store does not matter; keep it simple. Default HNSW settings can lose a third of the true neighbours
on clustered data: tune and measure recall before trusting a fast index. Random vectors are a hard case for HNSW, so part 2 is a
stress test, not a prediction for real files.

## 7. Analyze your bills: AI reads, code calculates (`rag/make_bills.py`, `rag/analyze.py`)

A fictional `Documents` folder: 12 monthly electricity bills (Nov 2025 to Oct 2026, inconsistent file names), 6 bank statements,
water and phone bills. The right answers are written to `rag/bills_truth.json` before any model sees the files.
EmbeddingGemma 2 finds the bills, Gemma 4 E4B reads ONE file at a time into JSON, plain Python adds and compares.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Electricity bills found | 12/12 |
| Each bill's total read exactly | 12/12 |
| 12-month total | exact ($1,741.64) |
| Naive way: all 12 bills in one prompt, "what is the total?" | $1,477.17 (wrong) |
| Bank statements: 6-month spend per category | 6/6 categories exact |

Savings sentences are built from the calculated numbers, not written by the model.
